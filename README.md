# Green Business Activity Dictionary

## Overview

This repository provides a comprehensive keyword-based **Green Business Activity Dictionary** designed to identify green business activities in corporate textual disclosures and business descriptions.

The dictionary is developed by combining terminology from established environmental taxonomies, climate-transition frameworks, industry classification systems, and the academic climate-finance literature. Its purpose is to provide a transparent and reproducible text-based approach for identifying business activities associated with the low-carbon transition.

---

## Dictionary Construction

To construct the Green Business Activity Dictionary, we draw on multiple external sources, including the **EU Taxonomy** (European Commission, 2020), the **IEA Net Zero by 2050 Roadmap** (International Energy Agency, 2021), the **FTSE Russell Green Revenues Classification** (FTSE Russell, 2024), the **IPCC AR6 Working Group III Mitigation of Climate Change framework** (IPCC, 2022), and relevant academic research on climate risk, environmental innovation, green technologies, and corporate responses to climate change.

The construction process also draws on terminology and conceptual definitions appearing in leading climate-finance studies, including Bolton and Kacperczyk (2021), Sautner et al. (2023), and Li et al. (2024), together with related work on green innovation, clean technologies, and climate-related corporate activities.

The objective is to align the dictionary with widely recognised definitions of environmentally sustainable and climate-related economic activities rather than relying on a single classification framework.

---

## Green Activity Categories

The dictionary spans six broad categories of green business activities.

| Category | Description | Illustrative Keywords |
|---|---|---|
| **Renewable Energy** | Technologies and activities related to renewable and low-carbon energy generation | solar, photovoltaic, wind turbine, biofuel, green hydrogen |
| **Energy Efficiency** | Technologies and activities aimed at reducing energy consumption or improving energy efficiency | smart grid, LED lighting, waste heat recovery, heat pumps |
| **Carbon Management** | Activities associated with reducing, managing, capturing, or offsetting greenhouse-gas emissions | decarbonization, carbon capture, net zero |
| **Enabling Technologies** | Technologies that support the development, deployment, or scaling of low-carbon activities | See dictionary |
| **Climate & ESG Services** | Professional and technical services supporting climate transition, sustainability, and ESG-related activities | See dictionary |
| **Other Climate-Related Activities** | Additional climate-related terminology not captured within the preceding categories | See dictionary |

The full set of keywords is provided in the dictionary file contained in this repository.

Each category is mapped to relevant external sources to maintain conceptual consistency with established definitions of green economic activities.

For example, the **Renewable Energy** category includes terms such as *solar*, *photovoltaic*, *wind turbine*, *biofuel*, and *green hydrogen*. The **Energy Efficiency** category includes terms such as *smart grid*, *LED lighting*, *waste heat recovery*, and *heat pumps*. Similarly, **Carbon Management** captures terminology such as *decarbonization*, *carbon capture*, and *net zero*.

The **Enabling Technologies** and **Climate & ESG Services** categories broaden the coverage of the dictionary by capturing technologies, innovations, and professional services that facilitate the transition toward a low-carbon economy.

---

## Intended Application

The dictionary is designed for text-based empirical research examining corporate environmental and climate-related activities.

Potential applications include the identification of green business activities in:

- corporate business descriptions;
- annual reports;
- sustainability and ESG reports;
- regulatory filings;
- merger and acquisition target descriptions;
- company websites; and
- other corporate textual disclosures.

Researchers can apply the dictionary to classify textual observations based on the presence of green-business terminology or incorporate the keywords into broader natural-language-processing and text-classification procedures.

---

## Methodological Motivation

Identifying green business activities from corporate text is challenging because firms frequently describe environmentally relevant activities using heterogeneous terminology.

A keyword-based dictionary provides a transparent approach in which the underlying classification criteria can be inspected, modified, and replicated.

By combining terminology from regulatory frameworks, industry classifications, international climate-transition reports, and academic research, this dictionary seeks to capture a broad range of activities associated with environmental innovation and the transition toward a low-carbon economy.
